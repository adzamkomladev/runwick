### 4.2 One cause behind the misses

Every miss above traces to the same limit G-1 found: on this host each job costs about 64 store round trips. At 5–7 ms per round trip through the Docker proxy, under load, that caps one replica at about 5 Dispatches or Finishes per second.

- P-05's 3v1 ratio is bound by per-replica store latency, which grows with replicas (Reserve + Begin + Finish 567 ms at 1 replica, 1,003 ms at 6). There is no shared lock bottleneck unconstrained.
- P-06s needs 16.7 materializations per second.
- P-09 at 100/s needs 100 Dispatches per second.

The PF work (`docs/plans/perf-plan-draft.md`) cuts the per-job round trips from 64 to 21 on its branch. It is not part of this gate (§6).

### 4.3 Rate windows (P-06r)

The rolling-window query over every grant (10 §1) found no scope above its limit in any window. With 8 scopes, each authorized job writes 8 grant rows (3.2 kB of WAL per grant). With 1 scope, it writes 1 (19.6 kB, dominated by the job's own writes). In churn (limit 1/s, offered 2/s), 1.6 Begins/s were deferred without a partial charge.

### 4.4 Capacity shares (P-04)

### 4.5 Scheduler (P-06s)

- **Steady phase.** The materializer kept up with about 23 % of the 15,000 due occurrences (p50 lag 173 s, p99 317 s; the p99 is a censored lower bound). Identities were clean: no duplicate instant, no job shared by two occurrences, no occurrence without a job.
- **Catch-up.** A per-second `catch_up` schedule was stopped for 1,000 s. After the restart, it materialized 512 of its instants (11:35:36 – 11:44:07), at most 32 per command. The bound held, and the schedule was not flagged for review. The episode did not close within the 60 min timeout because the steady minute load starved it. The bound and pacing are correct. The throughput is the §4.2 limit.
