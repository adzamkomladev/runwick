### 4.2 Cause of the threshold misses

All misses share one measured cause: **per-transaction store time under CPU starvation, multiplied by the serial claim.**

- **Each store command costs 150–330 ms (p50), against ~2 ms RTT.** On an idle path the five store transactions per job (Dispatch, Reserve, Begin, Finish, plus readiness probes) each run about ten round trips (BEGIN, timeouts, gates, receipt lookup, body, receipt finalization, COMMIT; 07 §2.5 note). Each round trip waits for a CPU slice on a host whose run queue is 2–3× its core count:
  - P-01 Reserve p50: 166–194 ms.
  - Begin p50: 146–171 ms.
  - Finish p50: 187–214 ms.
  - Dispatch p50: 148–278 ms.

  Lock waiters were 0 in every single-worker run. Query plans are index scans on `jobs_ready` with sub-millisecond execution (§4.7). So the database is not the bottleneck: the time goes to scheduling the client and server processes.
- **The serial claim caps admission.** With claim concurrency 1 and prefetch 0, one pool admits at most `C_claim = 1 / t_claim` jobs/s (§4.4): 3.1–3.7/s in P-01.
- **The pressure gate takes a large share of the remaining time.** Renewal and settlement items waited ≥ 1 s for a control slot often enough to stop admission for 426–585 s of each 895 s P-01 window (`controlWait` stops: 102–142 per run). This is the gate working as specified (05 §15): on a host this slow, control replies arrive late enough to trip it.
- **The producer is bounded locally.** At 100/s offered with dispatch p50 ≈ 0.15–0.28 s, two concurrent calls plus 64 waiting operations accept only 4–8/s. 91–95 % of arrivals return `local_capacity_exceeded`, known not submitted, as designed (CAP-11 b).

Profiles: process CPU, GC and timer lag are recorded per run (GC pause 0.1–0.4 %, allocation ≈ 233 KiB/job in P-01). The traces are the sample files `worker1.samples.jsonl`, `producer.samples.jsonl` and `db-samples.jsonl` in each run directory. The comparison point is the reference environment, which this host is not. Until a measurement on a dedicated host exists, the misses show that M2 cannot meet D-14 on a starved shared host. They do not show whether it meets D-14 on the reference environment.

### 4.5 Which bound limited P-02 (item 5)

- **The sustainable rate was 0.5/s.** The 1/s low-ramp step saturated: accepted 0.87/s of 0.96/s offered, finishes 0.86/s (< 98 %), backlog flat (1 → 1). At the 50/s first step: accepted 4.47/s, finishes 1.39/s, backlog 162 → 530.
- **Neither ceiling bound P-02.** `C_claim` (2.4–3.2/s) and the slot-bound ceiling (19–24/s) are both above the 0.5–1/s sustained. The **measured bottleneck is per-transaction latency under host CPU saturation**:
  - dispatch p95 1.4–1.9 s at 0.48/s offered;
  - the producer's two in-flight calls and 1 s admission wait reject arrivals (`local_capacity_exceeded`);
  - control-wait pressure stops for 52–103 s of each 15 min window.
- **The database itself was not saturated.** Lock waiters were 0. WAL was about 17.5 KiB/job at 2.1 receipt writes/s. The query plans are index-only and sub-millisecond.

At 50/s offered the binding bound is `C_claim`: finishes reached 1.39/s while the backlog grew.

### 4.6 SP-031 consequence (item 6)

Recommendation: **SP-031 is justified.** In every loaded run the serial claim is the hard ceiling: `C_claim` is about 3/s, 27–32× below the 100/s target. Both claim-side changes attack it directly:

- **Batched Reserve** (N jobs per claim transaction) multiplies `C_claim` by about N at nearly constant `t_claim`, because a transaction's cost here is dominated by its round trips, not its rows. At the measured `t_claim` of 0.27–0.32 s, reaching 100/s needs a batch of about 32. On a reference host with `t_claim` near 10–20 ms, a batch of 2–4 would do.
- **`ReserveAndBegin/1`** removes one sequential transaction per job. The store-time floor falls by `t_begin` (248–297 ms measured), from 815–965 ms to about 520–670 ms. The slot-bound ceiling rises from about 22–25 to about 30–35 jobs/s at fixed 16 with a 100 ms handler.
- **Batched Renew** matters for renewal waves. G1-M2 renews 16 leases about every 15 s and was still pressure-stopped 161 s of 895 s by `controlWait`. Batching turns 16 lane-limited calls (renewal lane 4) into a few transactions.

The expected gains scale with `t_reserve` and `t_begin` as measured on this host. They must be re-measured on the reference environment before SP-031 is sized. Whether SP-031 moves earlier than its planned slot is a planning decision for the next milestone, not part of G-1.

### 4.7 G1-M1, G1-M2, G1-M3 statements and query plans (item 7)

- **G1-M1 idle cost.** One worker, fixed 16, empty queue, 10 min:
  - 307 readiness probes over the whole run, about 0.5 probes/s.
  - Measured window: 2.73 transactions/s (this includes the sampler's own queries) and 0 receipt writes/s.
  - Connections: one work connection plus the sampler.
  - **Fix found:** before the SP-010 idle-backoff fix, the same workload ran about 30 probes/s (49 transactions/s). The opportunity loop's unused ticket woke the pool, and every 1 s pressure tick woke all pools. Both woke the idle backoff (100 ms → 2 s ± 20 %).
- **G1-M2 renewal margin.** 16 handlers of 120 s for 15 min at default timing:
  - Minimum renewal margin 39.0 s; p01 41.2 s; p50 45.1 s (n = 897 renewals).
  - 0 missed margins; **0 lease-safety losses**.
  - Control-I/O wait p99 2.26 s (threshold miss; cause §4.2); 32 `controlWait` pressure stops (161 s).
- **G1-M3 contention.** Two worker processes, fixed 8 each, one queue, type ceiling 4, no-op handler, at the P-02 sustainable rate (0.5/s), 15 min:
  - 0.48 finishes/s against 0.48/s offered; backlog flat.
  - Lock waiters: max 2, mean 0.04. `CapacityBlocked`: 0, because the type ceiling of 4 was never reached at this rate.

  Execution time is 0.06–3.4 ms. The 3.4 ms maximum was on the 160-job G1-M2 backlog, and every probe touched 1–5 shared buffers.

### 4.8 Run failures found and fixed

- **P-08c, run 1.** The checker recorded one correctness violation: "producer accepted 6250 dispatches but the store holds 6251 jobs".
  - The outage produced two `outcome_unknown` Dispatch results. The producer counted both as failures, but one had committed.
  - This is a checker defect: the contract says an outcome-unknown Dispatch may have applied and is resolved by resending the same command (CMD-02).
  - The store side was correct: 6251 jobs, each succeeded once, none executed twice.
  - Fixed in `d47b7a0` (07 I-16); P-08c was rerun in run 2.
- **G1-M3, run 1.** Worker 1 exited with code 134. Its sampler hit a race in `LeaseCoordinator.OldestWait`: the last waiter left between `IsEmpty` and `Max()`, and `Max()` threw.
  - The runner recorded the run as clean. The surviving worker and the store snapshot showed no violation, but half of the samples were missing.
  - The engine's 1 s pressure check uses the same read. A fault there ends the critical supervisor task, which closes admission: the worker stays safe but stops admitting. This is a liveness bug in the engine.
  - Fixed in `ab4989d`, with a Pure regression test (CAP-10) that fails on the old code.
  - A non-zero worker exit is now a run failure (07 I-17), and G1-M3 was rerun in run 2.
- **Postgres suite, run 2.** 36 of 116 tests failed: the connections of every concurrent test were refused or reset, because the new CAP-11 (c) outage test called `RestartStore(Kill, 60 s)` on the shared server outside the `StoreRestart` collection that 07 §2.3 requires.
  - In the same run, CAP-10 recorded one reservation 67 ms after a pressure stop: the claim already in flight when the gate closed. The pool checks the gate before every Reserve and claims serially, so at most that one claim can land.
  - Both fixed in `708e200` (test-only); the suites were rerun as run 3.
- **Postgres suite, run 3.** Three tests failed on their own timing, not on engine behaviour (07 I-18, C-9). All were fixed in `da9c14e` and the suites rerun as run 4.
  - **PGX-04 (b)** saw one transient conflict in 100 opposite-order batches. The assertion used the runner's transient-conflict counter, which also counts 55P03, and a 2 s `lock_timeout` behind a slow commit on this host is not a deadlock. The deadlock tests (PGX-04, BATCH-03, ADM-03, TX-03) now count 40P01 alone.
  - **OWN-01:** the Renew, sent 1.5 s before expiry, committed after it. The test now renews well inside a 10 s lease and releases the held Recover only after the original expiry.
  - **CAP-10:** the events were read while the last pressure stop was still in force, before it could resume.
    - Rerunning it exposed a second issue in its no-latency phase. With no fault injected, a wave of 16 renewals on 4 lanes queued past the 1 s `controlWait` stop, because a Renew takes 0.5–1 s on this host.
    - That phase now measures the Renew round trip. The strict "no stop" assertion applies only where three Renews fit well inside the threshold; otherwise every stop must gate and resume correctly (07 C-9).
- **Postgres suite, run 4, and repeated ownership runs.** Three more test preconditions failed under load. All were fixed in `2a29ca5` (test-only; 07 I-18) and the suites rerun as run 5.
  - **OWN-01:** the run-3 fix held the Recover's open transaction for about 10 s, which hit `idle_in_transaction_session_timeout` (10 s). The lease is now 6 s.
  - **DEP-04 (b):** the cancel, sent a fixed 300 ms after the COMMIT was released, reached the server before it ran the COMMIT, so the server cancelled the COMMIT. The runner correctly reported that rollback as not applied, but the case needs a cancel while the reply is in flight. (b) and (d) now cancel or sever only once the commit is visible to another session, with the reply held 10 s.
  - **OWN-02:** reserving with a 1 s lease and then beginning took more than 1 s, so A's attempt was abandoned before start. The test now uses 3 s and asserts that Begin applied.
- **Postgres suite, run 5.** The host's other tenants kept the load average at 15–16, and the suite took 1411 s. Nine tests failed: lost leases, re-executions, exhausted budgets and expired test leases. All are consistent with Renew round trips of 1.1 s against the 1.25 s control timeout of the ÷4 timing. No code changed; the suites were rerun on a quiet host as run 6.
- **Postgres suite, run 6.** Both fixes are in `b381b63` (test-only; 07 I-18, C-9), and the suites were rerun as run 7.
  - **WRK-08:** the 20 s Kill outage lasted 41.7 s. After minutes of suite traffic, PostgreSQL's WAL replay on restart took about 20 s, so the Finishes outlived their 30 s lifetime. The harness now runs `CHECKPOINT` before the kill.
  - **CAP-10:** its no-latency phase recorded one `lag` stop, a timer-lag stop caused by the suite's own parallel load. The store-bound branch now accepts `controlWait` or `lag` stops, provided each one gates and resumes correctly.
- **Re-execution in P-08a and P-08b.** One job in each ran twice. In both, the first execution's Finish expired without a receipt while the 1 s or 5 s latency was on ("unknown durability … recovery applies"), so recovery returned the job and it ran again.
  - This is the documented at-least-once path. The reference model accepted both histories.
  - P-08b's `lost:BeginUnresolved` attempt resolved late as `CommandExpired` and was never invoked.

## 6. Fixes found during SP-010

- **Engine idle backoff** (`b69f587`): an unused claim ticket raised the refill wake, and every pressure tick woke the pools. Idle probing fell from about 30/s to about 0.5/s (G1-M1).
- **`LeaseCoordinator.OldestWait` race** (`ab4989d`): see §4.8.
- **libfaketime image:** a global `LD_PRELOAD` deadlocked `gosu` at container start. The image now wraps only the `postgres` binary, which made TIME-13 (c) and ENC-01 (a) runnable.
- **`RestartStore` timing:** downtime now counts from the kill. The stop and start go through the Docker CLI, with readiness polled directly every 250 ms. The Testcontainers wait strategy had stretched a 20 s outage to 40 s.
- **Test hardening for full-suite load:** CMD-05 lifetime, TIME-12 timing and preconditions, the WRK-16 measurement window, the END-01 drop target, and the TIME-11 hold flag.
- **Workload runner:** outcome-unknown recovery in the producer (I-16) and the worker-crash rule (I-17).
- **Test timing under host load** (`da9c14e`, `2a29ca5`, `b381b63`): checkpoint before a Kill restart, 40P01-only deadlock evidence, OWN-01/OWN-02 lease margins, DEP-04 cancel and sever only after the commit is visible, and the CAP-10 event read and no-latency phase (I-18, C-9).
